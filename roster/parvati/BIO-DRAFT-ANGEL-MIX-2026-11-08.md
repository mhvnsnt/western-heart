## Identity (addition to canon)

MEGAMI — the Shrine Soul, the guardian of the sealed door. What the older
canon did not say, because nobody who wrote it had stood in front of her:
she is **tall past the point of politeness** — the tallest soul the Trial has
ever pulled, head and shoulders over the fighters who come to test her door.
People crane their necks at the shrine. She has never once bent hers to meet
them. The door chose a guardian the size of a monument, and she carries the
size the way the shrine carries its stone: without apology, without effort,
as simple fact.

## Presence — the heat she brings

The shrine stands in the far southern country, where the heat comes off the
stone in waves and never fully leaves it, even at night. Megami is of that
heat. Where the old canon called her grief *structural*, this is its
weather: a slow, heavy warmth that fills a room before she speaks. She does
not perform confidence — confidence is for people who can imagine being
refused. The door has never opened for anyone. She simply occupies ground
the way a procession occupies a street: fully, unhurriedly, daring the
world to ask her to move.

Her boldness is not volume. It is **scale**. A laugh, when it comes, is
full-bodied and unguarded — the one crack where the monument shows the
woman — and then the stone settles again. Fighters who meet her remember
two things: how far up they had to look, and how quiet she was while they
were looking.

## The ink — the shrine's ledger

Her tattoos are not decoration. They are the shrine's ledger, written on the
only surface that has never left the door: her own skin. All of it black and
grey, the colour of old stone and ash.

- **The butterflies (both front thighs — six on the right, five on the
  left).** One for every soul who came to the sealed door and was turned
  away gently. They are small because mercy, at the shrine, was small and
  specific. They cluster on her thighs because she sat at the door for
  centuries, and the turned-away left their marks where her hands rested.
- **The long dark piece (outer left thigh, hip to knee).** The one soul she
  did not turn away. It runs the full length of her thigh because Chatur
  stayed the longest. The shrine does not say her name in this entry. The
  ink knows.
- **The wings (upper back, blade to blade).** The sealed door has no wings;
  its guardian grew them instead. Feathered, heavy, folded — wings that
  have never flown, because flying would mean leaving the post. They span
  her whole back so that anyone standing behind her understands: this is
  not a woman in front of a door. This is the door, standing up.
- **The lower back and the full backside coverage.** The oldest ink, from
  before the ledger had rules — dense, swirling, hip to hip and down the
  backs of her thighs. It is the shrine's foundation script: the vows under
  all the other vows, written where the stone meets the earth. Even Megami
  no longer reads it aloud.
- **The right arm.** Shoulder to mid-arm, a feathered wing-line — the one
  wing she allows in front of her, the door's edge made visible. Below it,
  in fine dark script, her philosophy, two lines: ***"The shrine was here
  before you. / It will be here after."*** On the inner forearm, a dark
  vertical piece, wrist to mid-forearm: the threshold stone, the weight she
  carries into every parry.
- **The hands.** Her pronouncement, split across them — ***koko*** on the
  left hand, ***made*** on the right, tiny script at the backs of the
  wrists. *Koko made.* This far. No further. An opponent close enough to
  read both hands has already been counted, and her counts do not go high.
- **The small word above the left thigh's hemline — *shizuka*.** Quiet.
  From her win pronouncement: *"Tobira wa, shizuka"* — the door is quiet.
  It is the smallest ink she carries and the last one she earned.
