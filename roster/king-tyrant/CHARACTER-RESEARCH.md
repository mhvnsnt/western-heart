## The "Black Snow" moment — what it actually was

- In TNA, Booker T did a **commentary stint as "Black Snow"** — a one-day
  commentary persona he invented on the spot.
- It went **viral**: a fan-run clip account (@bookercomstan, July 2020) started
  posting his commentary moments ("he narrates the action like only Booker T
  can"), and the clips spread widely.
- Booker addressed it himself on his *Hall of Fame* podcast (Fightful, ~2020):
  > "Whoever put it up there, needs to take it down. They're gonna get sued,
  > they're trying to get me sued, because **Black Snow is a real person. I stole
  > the persona for one day and put it on television, and next thing you know,
  > it's gone viral.**"
- The moment is pure Booker T: total confidence, zero shame, instantly turning
  a throwaway gag into something the whole internet quotes. That energy is what
  the name carries — not a costume, a *persona*.

Source: https://www.fightful.com/wrestling/booker-t-comments-commentary-twitter-account-says-he-got-heat-during-commentary-days/

## Booker T — style and vibe (documented)

- **The showman.** Five-time WCW World Champion (he says it as "Five Time... Five
  Time... Five Time... Five Time... FIVE TIME WCW CHAMPION!!!!" — the count
  itself is the catchphrase). Two-time WWE Hall of Famer. 35 championships
  across WCW, WWE, TNA.
- **The catchphrases.** "Can you dig that, SUCKA!", "Shucky Ducky Quack Quack!",
  "Tell me... he didn't just say that." On commentary: "Oh my GOODNESS!",
  "What da hale?!", "You Gotta Love It!", "with the greatest of ease!"
  (TV Tropes, Booker T entry).
- **The confidence.** Early in WCW he told the locker room straight up he was a
  top-five worker in the world — "I could be one. I could be five." Rubbed
  veterans the wrong way; he did not care (sacnilk, via his Hall of Fame
  podcast, Oct 2026).
- **The live-TV fearlessness.** The infamous 1997 Spring Stampede promo vs. Hulk
  Hogan — caught up in the moment, dropped a slur live on air, instantly knew it,
  kept going. He later called it "the worst moment in my life" and thought his
  career was over. It wasn't — he became the biggest Black star of his era
  (411mania, via his Hall of Fame show).
- **The barrier-breaker.** Openly unsure WCW would ever accept a Black world
  champion: "Because I was black, to be honest. I knew how good I was... you
  still wonder if that company would be willing to accept a black champion."
  (VICE "Who Killed WCW?", via Fightful). Then won it five times.
- **The spin-a-rooni.** His signature in-ring showboat — the breakdance spin
  that said "I'm better than you and I know it" without a word.
- **The commentator.** Joined WWE's desk in 2011, NXT's since 2022 — authentic,
  funny, passionate, never the polished corporate voice. Fans made him a meme;
  he leaned in.

## What this means for King Tyrant (direction notes, owner decides)

- King Tyrant is **Eddy Gordo-based** (capoeira) — the Booker T fold-in is VIBE,
  not moveset: the showman's entrance, the crown/cape regality of Attire 2
  ("Tyrant King"), the viral-persona confidence of Attire 3 ("Black Snow").
- The **crown + ermine cape** (Attire 2) reads "King Booker" directly — the
  self-crowned five-time champ energy.
- The **red robe + leopard collar** (Attire 3) reads Harlem Heat-era flash — the
  pimp-walk showman before the match even starts.
- Suggested canon thread (owner's call): King Tyrant talks about HIMSELF the way
  Booker narrated matches as Black Snow — third-person, outsized, funny, never
  doubting. The "Black Snow" name is a persona he wears, not a costume.
